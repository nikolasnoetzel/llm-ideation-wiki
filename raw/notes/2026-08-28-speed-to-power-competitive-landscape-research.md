# Raw note — Speed-to-power competitive landscape (Europe / Germany / Schleswig-Holstein)

**Originating question:** Who occupies which layer of the "speed-to-power" / powered-land data center site-development stack in Europe (esp. Germany), and is anyone already doing powered-land development in Schleswig-Holstein / northern Germany — and does kill criterion #2 (operators pay a premium for energized/grid-secured sites in secondary German locations) survive?

## 2. Schleswig-Holstein / northern Germany: powered-land activity EXISTS

**UNIQ LAND Development GmbH is already doing exactly the powered-land play in Schleswig-Holstein.**

- A 150MW data center campus (option to expand connection to 300MW) is planned by "developers Uniq Land" on a ~55,000 sqm site outside Langenhorn, North Frisia, Schleswig-Holstein, "close to the Danish border, about 140 km northwest of Hamburg." Source: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/150mw-data-center-planned-for-north-frisia-germany-attracts-protests/ (DCD; the bebeez.eu syndication dated 2026-07-01); also https://baxtel.com/news/150mw-data-center-planned-in-north-frisia-germany-draws-protests and https://www.datacenter-insider.de/300-mw-rechenzentrums-campus-in-nordfriesland-geplant-a-510040b38fe786bdc5460afb048fe8f7/ ("300-MW-Rechenzentrums-Campus in Nordfriesland geplant") [content not fetchable — egress blocked; headline only].
- Business model, per search extraction of Oliver Keim's LinkedIn profile (https://www.linkedin.com/in/oliver-keim-2337a782/): "UNIQ LAND was founded in 2026 by Oliver Keim and Jens Kampkötter... develops data center sites to shovel-ready status – location, grid connection, permitting." Based in Grünwald, Bavaria. That is a textbook powered-land developer, and their first announced site is in Nordfriesland. [uncertain: LinkedIn self-description, not independently verified.]
- Rationale, per DCD search extraction: "Uniq Land's managing director, Jens Kampkötter, cited Schleswig-Holstein's status as one of Germany's leading states when it comes to the generation of renewable energy" — i.e. the wind-surplus thesis is already being executed by someone else.
- **Friction datapoint:** "The project received a hostile reception at a town hall meeting on June 18 [2026]... municipal planning permission has yet to be granted." Residents objected to 27.5m building heights and process transparency (DCD/baxtel, above; also https://data-central.co.uk/north-frisia-says-not-so-fast/). Local opposition in rural SH is a real execution risk for anyone in this niche.
- Site listed at https://www.datacentermap.com/germany/langenhorn/uniq-langenhorn-dc1/ ("UNIQ Langenhorn DC1").

- **Green Flexibility (Kempten, DE)** — BESS developer/operator, founded 2023, team incl. ex-Sonnen founder Christoph Ostermann. Partners Group committed "up to EUR 400 million" equity plus debt, "giving green flexibility more than EUR 1 billion to invest." Sources: https://www.partnersgroup.com/en/news-and-views/press-releases/investment-news/detail?news_id=b7221595-824a-4afc-915e-1b5afada974a ; https://www.ess-news.com/2025/01/17/germanys-green-flexibility-secures-up-to-eur-400m-equity-finance-for-grid-scale-batteries/ (2025-01-17). Directly relevant: "Green Flexibility proposes template for flexible grid connection deals" (https://www.ess-news.com/2026/07/03/green-flexibility-proposes-template-for-flexible-grid-connection-deals/, 2026-07-03) — they are standardizing the flexible-connection layer in Germany, though from the BESS side, not the DC side.
- **Flexible Connection Agreements (FCAs) in Germany** — regulatory substrate now exists: EU directive implemented via EEG/EnWG reform "came into force in January 2025"; FCAs "trade firm (unrestricted) grid rights for faster access." Sources: https://modoenergy.com/research/en/germany-november-2025-flexible-connection-agreements-fcas-regulatory-reform (Nov 2025); https://www.gridcog.com/blog/how-flexible-connection-agreements-fca-transform-grid-access-for-german-battery-projects ; Norton Rose Fulbright explicitly frames the reform as "Grid connections for BESS **and data centers** reimagined" (https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ddde3596/grid-connections-for-bess-and-data-centers-reimagined). Most FCA commentary is BESS-focused; a DC-focused FCA player appears to be white space.
- **NDC-GARBE Data Centers Europe** (Hamburg-rooted Garbe + NDC JV) — "replicable solution which seamlessly bundles all processes from site sourcing to fast delivery," leveraging Garbe's landbank; pipeline projects in Munich, Frankfurt, first project Hanau. ## 6. KILL CRITERION #2 — do buyers pay a premium for energized/grid-secured sites in secondary locations?

Evidence FOR survival:
- **Powered land has a hard, published price:** "Prime powered land in Europe now costs €2.26m per megawatt, up 82% since 2021, on JLL's figures" — and JLL's later figures show "core markets averaging €2.36 million per megawatt of IT load, secondary markets like Copenhagen, Warsaw and Milan at €978,000, and tertiary areas such as Bordeaux at €512,000" (primary = 1.7x secondary, 3.1x tertiary). Sources: https://thenextweb.com/news/europe-data-centres-leaving-flap-d-grid-queues-land ; https://www.jll.com/en-uk/insights/emea-data-centre-report (mid-year 2026). Note: this says powered land is *valued per MW at all* in secondary markets (~€1M/MW) — the product category has a price, which is the core requirement.
- **Demand is structurally moving to secondary sites:** "63% of Europe's new data centre capacity is now going somewhere other than the big five" (thenextweb, above); "Between 2026 and 2028 the average distance of the sites from a major hub will be 175 kilometers... compared with 46 kilometers for projects delivered between 2022 and 2025" (https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/8yp9kdcjh); "secondary markets growing at twice the rate of traditional FLAP-D hubs" (https://www.computeforecast.com/blogs/flap-d-collapse-europe-ai-data-center-expansion/); "over half of AI growth is expected in Tier 2 and emerging markets" (JLL via search extraction).
- **Grid queue is the binding constraint:** "In the FLAP-D markets, new facilities wait on average 7 to 10 years for a grid connection, rising to 13 years in the most congested primary markets" (https://datacentrereview.com/2026/08/ai-boom-pushes-hyperscale-data-centres-away-from-europes-major-hubs/, Aug 2026); "Grid readiness — not capital availability — is the binding constraint" (computeforecast, above).
- **Latency-insensitivity of training confirmed:** "AI training requirements of 100 MW and above are not latency-sensitive and follow available power instead"; "Training workloads can tolerate delays of up to 100 milliseconds between adjacent regions, which allows operators to site them in remote, power-rich areas" (https://build.inc/insights/training-vs-inference-data-center-design-differences ; https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/opinions/training-built-the-campuses-inference-will-choose-the-markets/).
- **Someone is already underwriting the SH thesis with money:** UNIQ LAND's Langenhorn 150–300MW project (section 2) exists precisely because of SH wind surplus.

Evidence AGAINST / caveats:
- The 1.7–2.3x premium runs the *other* way: primary-market powered land is worth more than secondary — i.e. an energized SH site is worth roughly €1M/MW-class, not Frankfurt's €2.3M/MW. The margin thesis must rest on *creation cost vs. secondary-market powered price*, not on secondary sites out-pricing Frankfurt. No Germany-specific secondary powered-land *transaction* comps were found in searches (searches returned pan-European averages only — explicitly noted: nothing useful found on actual German secondary-market powered-land deal prices).
- Local opposition can strand the asset pre-permit (Langenhorn town hall, 2026-06-18).
- 72% of German capacity is still Rhine-Main (Cushman & Wakefield H1 2025) — buyer depth in northern Germany is unproven; Langenhorn has no announced anchor tenant in the results found.

**VERDICT: SURVIVES (with a reframing).** Operators demonstrably pay for grid-secured land as a distinct asset class (€/MW pricing exists across market tiers, up 82% since 2021), demand growth is disproportionately in secondary/tier-2 locations because 7–13-year FLAP-D queues make power the scarce input, and AI training's latency-insensitivity removes the FLAP requirement for the largest workloads. What is NOT supported: the notion that secondary German sites price *above* primary ones — the premium is for *powered vs. unpowered*, not *secondary vs. primary*. Kill criterion #2 should be restated as "spread between site-acquisition + energization cost and secondary-market powered-land value (~€1M/MW EU average)". Remaining open risk: zero German secondary-market transaction comps found, and no proven exit (sale to operator) for a northern-German powered site yet.

### Synthesis

- The stack in Germany is real but young. The pure powered-land layer has named, funded entrants (UNIQ LAND explicitly in Schleswig-Holstein; Gridfield in Munich with a broader charter; NDC-GARBE as the landbank incumbent) — so "nobody is doing this in northern Germany" is **false as of mid-2026**. First-mover claim is gone; fast-follower with differentiated angle (site-intelligence software, FCA structuring for DCs, BESS-co-located flexible connections) is the honest positioning.
- The most open layers in Germany: (a) GridCARE-style power-acceleration software (nothing found) and DC-specific flexible-connection structuring (FCA law in force since Jan 2025, templates only now emerging from the BESS side via Green Flexibility).
- Gridfield is essentially invisible publicly (registry entry only, Dec 2025 vintage); its €15M/€50M numbers are unverifiable hearsay. That itself is information: pre-launch, no announced sites, no public northern-Germany footprint.
- Contradiction to carry: JLL premium multiples appear as both "2.3x/4x" (older figure via thenextweb) and "1.7x/3.1x" (JLL mid-2026) — different report vintages; use the 2026 JLL figures.
- Execution risk validated by Langenhorn: rural SH municipalities can and do resist 27m halls; permitting/community work is part of the product, not overhead.
