## 1. Netzpaket status (cabinet 29 July 2026 → Bundestag)

- Cabinet adopted the "Netzpaket" (Gesetz zur Änderung des Energiewirtschaftsrechts zur Synchronisierung des Anlagenzubaus mit dem Netzausbau und zur Verbesserung des Netzanschlussverfahrens) together with the EEG-2027-Novelle on **29 July 2026**.
  - Source: https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/kabinett-eeg-novelle-netzpaket-strom-2448636 (Jul 2026); https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/07/29/kabinett-beschliesst-entwuerfe-zu-eeg-2027-und-netzpaket/ (2026-07-29).
- Parliamentary track: "Bundestag and Bundesrat will hold consultations after the summer recess … parliamentary consultation is scheduled for September 2026 … planned entry into force is January 1, 2027" [search-extract]. Driver of the timeline is the expiring EU state-aid approval of the EEG at end of 2026. Changes in the parliamentary process are explicitly expected.
  - Sources: https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/29/kabinett-bringt-eeg-2027-novelle-und-netzpaket-auf-den-weg/ (2026-07-29); https://www.arqis.com/en/arqis-blog/eeg-novelle-und-netzanschlusspaket-im-kabinett/ (2026).
  - A Bundestag Drucksache 21/7734 dated 26.08.2026 appeared in results (https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/077/2107734.pdf) — likely the bill reaching the Bundestag; content not verified [uncertain: PDF not fetched].
- What §17(2b) EnWG / §8a EEG anchor: FCAs were NOT created by the Netzpaket — they were introduced by the **Solarspitzengesetz, in force since February 2025**: "Flexible grid connection agreements were introduced in the 'Solar Peak Act' (February 21, 2025) in § 8a EEG 2023 and § 17 Abs. 2b EnWG" [search-extract]. In an FCA, connectee and grid operator agree static or dynamic power limits to use spare capacity and get connected earlier.
  - Sources: https://www.roedl.com/insights/netzpaket-bmwe-referentenentwurf-netzanschluss-redispatch-2-0-ee-projekte/ ; https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/02/20/netzpaket-was-der-referentenentwurf-fuer-netzanschluss-redispatch-2-0-und-erneuerbaren-projekte-bedeuten-koennte/ (2026-02-20).
- Scope of §17(2b): "Section 17(2b) EnWG covers all grid users such as battery storage systems or data centres, and allows for both feed-in capacity and offtake capacity to be limited" [search-extract from Norton Rose Fulbright piece].
  - Source: https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/ddde3596/grid-connections-for-bess-and-data-centers-reimagined (2026; fetch blocked).
- What the Netzpaket adds on top (new **§17b EnWG-E**): prioritization of connection requests replacing first-come-first-served. "Eine Priorisierung kann durch ein zeitweises Freihalten von Netzanschlusskapazitäten oder im Abschluss flexibler Netzanschlussvereinbarungen erfolgen" [search-extract]. Criteria include "security and reliability of the electricity supply system, existing statutory targets for the expansion of generation plants, energy storage facilities **and consumers**, assumptions from the scenario framework approved by the Federal Network Agency … efficient use of network connection points" [search-extract]. TSOs must develop a joint, transparent, non-discriminatory procedure approved by BNetzA. "Sektorspezifische Annahmen und Ziele wie … die Ansiedlung von Rechenzentren sind berücksichtigungsfähig" [search-extract] — i.e., data-center siting goals are an admissible prioritization consideration.
  - Sources: https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/netzpaket-2026-neue-regeln-fuer-netzanschluesse-geplant (2026); https://www.advant-beiten.com/en/news/das-bmwe-netzpaket-2026-zentrale-neuerungen-und-erste-bewertung (2026); https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/das-netzanschlusspaket ; Referentenentwurf PDF (Bearbeitungsstand 13.01.2026): https://klimareporter.de/images/dokumente/2026/02/referentenentwurf-netzanschlusspaket.pdf

## 3. Do flexible connections / prioritization apply to large NEW LOADS? (Kill criterion #1)

- Statute: §17(2b) EnWG is technology-neutral. "This option exists according to § 17 Abs. 2b EnWG in a technology-neutral manner for all categories of connecting parties whose connection requests would otherwise be rejected" [search-extract]; it covers "generation, consumption, or storage" systems and allows limiting **maximum consumption** capacity.
  - Sources: https://www.leitfeld-recht.de/de/meldung/46/flexible-netzanschlussvereinbarungen-%E2%80%93-ein-mittel-zur ; Stiftung Umweltenergierecht presentation (2025-09-22): https://stiftung-umweltenergierecht.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Franke_Flexible_Netzanschlussvereinbarungen_BNK_2025-09-22.pdf ; Norton Rose Fulbright (URL above).
- TSO practice already includes loads: the four German TSOs' **Reifegradverfahren** ("first ready, not first come") started **1 April 2026** with a first information and application phase, explicitly for "large battery storage systems, data centers, electrolyzers and other large consumers"; allocation by maturity "including a network and system benefit component" when requests exceed capacity [search-extract].
  - Sources: https://www.tennet.eu/de/news/uebertragungsnetzbetreiber-fuehren-reifegradverfahren-fuer-netzanschlussantraege-von-speichern-und-grossverbrauchern-ein (2026); TSO joint documentation PDF (2026-02-05): https://www.netztransparenz.de/Portals/1/Dokumente/Presse/2026/2026-02-05_Vier_Uebertragungsnetzbetreiber_Reifegradverfahren_Dokumentation_V100.pdf ; https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/energy/2026-posts/from-first-come-to-first-ready-germanys-new-maturity-based-grid-connection-process-for-bess-and-large-scale-consumers
- Practitioner literature specifically addresses data-center FCAs: "Netzanschluss von Rechenzentren: Reifegradverfahren, Netzpaket und FCA – was Betreiber wissen müssen" https://www.anwalt.de/rechtstipps/netzanschluss-von-rechenzentren-reifegradverfahren-netzpaket-und-fca-was-betreiber-wissen-muessen-273695.html (fetch blocked) and https://www.energieundrecht.com/blog/netzanschluss-rechenzentren-reifegradverfahren
- Counterweight: every signed FCA found to date is a battery, DSO template pages are filed under "Energie einspeisen" (feed-in), and BNetzA's standardizing Festlegung only comes in 2027. [uncertain: no documented load-side FCA signing found — absence of evidence in search results, not proof none exists.]

**VERDICT (kill #1): SURVIVES.** The statute (§17(2b) EnWG, in force since Feb 2025) is explicitly technology-neutral and covers offtake/consumption limits; the Netzpaket's §17b EnWG-E prioritization criteria expressly include consumers and data-center siting goals; and the TSOs' Reifegradverfahren has covered data centers since April 2026. But de-facto FCA practice is ~100% storage today, so a load-side FCA in 2026 means being a first mover negotiating bilaterally with SH Netz/TenneT rather than signing a standard product; standardized terms realistically arrive with the BNetzA Festlegung in 2027 — earlier than a "not before 2028" kill, but not off-the-shelf now.

## 4. Did the cabinet version water down load-side flexible connections? (Kill criterion #3)

- The evolution went the other way: the government draft (17 July 2026 version) "defused" the most controversial pro-grid-operator elements — "the originally very far-reaching and highly controversial redispatch reservation and the possibility of designating capacity-limited network areas being 'defused' in multiple respects … significant adjustments were made in favor of project developers and operators" [search-extract]. The draft is described as "a compromise proposal that addresses and implements criticism points from the industry."
  - Sources: https://www.roedl.com/insights/netzpaket-light-referentenentwurf/ ("Netzpaket Light?", 2026); https://www.cleanthinking.de/netzpaket-kabinett-kritik/ (2026); https://www.green-2-market.de/netzpaket-redispatch-solarinvestment/
- No source found reporting that load-side flexible connections were stripped or narrowed between Referentenentwurf and Kabinettsentwurf. FCAs themselves are already binding law (Feb 2025) independent of the Netzpaket, so even a Netzpaket failure would not remove the FCA instrument — it would only remove the prioritization/reservation superstructure.
- Residual risk: parliamentary changes in Sept 2026 are "explicitly expected" (solarserver.de, 2026-07-29), and the criticism dynamic in the Bundestag runs mostly on EEG/redispatch issues, not on load flexibility. [uncertain: final Bundestag text does not exist yet; Drucksache 21/7734 unverified.]

**VERDICT (kill #3): SURVIVES (no evidence of watering down).** Cabinet-stage changes softened grid-operator powers, not connectee flexibility; the FCA legal basis predates and is independent of the Netzpaket. Residual legislative risk until the Sept 2026 Bundestag vote and 1 Jan 2027 entry into force.

## Contradictions across sources

1. **FCA as accelerant vs. value-destroyer**: law firms and the data-center strategy frame FCAs as the fast-connection tool; Modo Energy quantifies worst-case FCAs as destroying 20% of lifetime revenue / 5pp IRR (for BESS), and Green Flexibility says DSO drafts "restrict … instead of enabling." For a data center (paying for reliability, not arbitraging spreads) the economics differ from BESS, but the DSO-drafting-behavior warning carries over.
2. **SH curtailment as local opportunity vs. accounting artifact**: SH reports 802 GWh onshore curtailed, but >65% is at TenneT's request for congestion partly *outside* SH — so siting a load in SH doesn't mechanically absorb "SH's" curtailed power; benefit depends on where behind which constraint you sit.
3. **"Grid full until mid-2030s" (Mainova/Frankfurt) vs. "large consumers have a real chance" (SH minister)**: consistent once regionalized — the scarcity is in southern/Frankfurt load centers, the surplus in the north — and this asymmetry is precisely the venture's premise.

### Synthesis

The regulatory architecture the venture needs exists or is arriving on a knowable schedule: FCAs are binding law since Feb 2025 and technology-neutral (consumption caps expressly allowed); the TSO Reifegradverfahren has been allocating capacity to data centers by maturity + system benefit since April 2026; the Netzpaket (Bundestag Sept 2026, in force target 1 Jan 2027) adds §17b prioritization in which data-center siting is an admissible criterion; BNetzA standardizes FCAs/BKZ in 2027. SH is arguably the best market in Germany for this play: multi-hundred-GWh curtailment, a state government actively marketing grid headroom to large consumers, and live precedents (Langenhorn 300 MW, Schuby, Lyten-Heide including a data center). The venture's real risks are not legal but practical: (a) no load-side FCA has demonstrably been signed yet — the developer would be negotiating first-of-a-kind terms with SH Netz/TenneT, and DSO drafting behavior on the storage side has been restrictive; (b) the flagship stranded asset (Northvolt Heide) is already taken by Lyten — partly for a data center, which validates demand but means speed now comes from the Reifegradverfahren cycles and bilateral FCA negotiation, not asset acquisition; (c) prioritization under §17b runs through a BNetzA-approved TSO procedure, i.e., DSO/TSO discretion remains the gatekeeper, and "maturity" (land, permits, financing) is the currency — which is exactly what a speed-to-power developer can manufacture.

**KILL CRITERION #1 (FCA/prioritization applies to large new loads?): SURVIVES** — §17(2b) EnWG is technology-neutral and covers consumption limits; Reifegradverfahren covers data centers since April 2026; §17b criteria include consumers. Caveat: zero documented load-side FCA signings to date; standardized load FCAs realistically 2027 (BNetzA Festlegung), not 2028+.

**KILL CRITERION #3 (final law strips load-side flexibility?): SURVIVES** — cabinet-stage changes weakened grid-operator powers (redispatch reservation "defused"), not connectee flexibility; FCA legal basis predates the Netzpaket and stands regardless. Residual [uncertain] until the Bundestag vote (Sept 2026): final text not yet adopted.
